## What I changed from your text, and why (all for accuracy)
- **"Almost impossible in America" → "hard to find in America".** UPI-style instant payments exist in the US too (e.g. Zelle, FedNow), they are just not used at the chai-stall level. "Hard to find" is true; "impossible" is not.
- **"People pay over $100 a month and struggle to get a single bar of signal" removed.** Our sources show single-line plans from the big carriers start around $45–50, premium ones around $70–80, and cheap prepaid options start at $25. The "$100" and "no signal" claims are not supported, so I did not script them.
- **"Less than the price of a coffee" → "a few hundred rupees a month".** Jio's cheapest unlimited 5G plan is ₹198 for 14 days; Airtel's is ₹379 (capped at 300 GB per 30 days). Roughly a coffee, but it depends on the exchange rate and the café, so we show the rupee price instead.
- **Medicine.** Indian law lists many drugs (Schedule H, H1, antibiotics among them) as prescription-only, but enforcement is lax and pharmacies often sell without one. We say "many Indian pharmacies sell common medicines", not "you can buy anything", and line 8 adds the honest catch. Also "hundreds of dollars" became "about 170 dollars" (the averages we found are $40–300, average $171).
- **Title.** "Better" is an opinion; line 9 balances it with "what America does better". The channel rule is no politics jabs, so the tone stays friendly and fair.
