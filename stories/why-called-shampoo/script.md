# Why is it called shampoo? (Doodle Whys, Part 2)

## Script (one line = one picture)
1. **Hook:** Why is it called shampoo? *(big text "SHAMPOO?", Sticky scratching his head, bottle pops in)*
2. Because it is a Hindi word. It comes from "champo", which means to press or massage. *(word card: champo = press, massage)*
3. In India, champo was a relaxing head massage with oil. *(stick figure massaging a head, oil drops)*
4. In the 1700s, the word reached English, and it still meant massage. *(arrow India to England, word hops across)*
5. In 1814, an Indian man named Sake Dean Mahomed opened a bathhouse in Brighton, England, offering shampooing. *(name label, small bathhouse, "Brighton 1814")*
6. It was so popular that two British kings made him their Shampooing Surgeon. *(two crowns, title card)*
7. Only around the 1860s did shampoo start to mean washing your hair. *(timeline: massage then hair wash)*
8. So every wash, you are saying a Hindi word. Next: another Hindi word hiding in English. *(Sticky with shampoo bottle, teaser)*

## Sources and fact check
1. Wikipedia, "Shampoo" (etymology section), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shampoo . Word from Hindi *chāmpō* (imperative of *chāmpnā*, "to press, knead, massage"); first recorded in English 1762; original sense was a head massage; the "wash the hair" sense came later (about 1860). It cites the American Heritage Dictionary (4th ed.), *Hobson-Jobson*, BBC News and Etymonline.
2. Wikipedia, "Sake Dean Mahomed", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake_Dean_Mahomed . "Mahomed's Baths" opened in 1814 in Brighton, described as the first commercial "shampooing" vapour bath in England; "shampooing" meant Indian massage ("champi"); appointed Shampooing Surgeon to George IV and William IV. It cites Teltscher (2000), Mahomed's own 1838 *Shampooing*, and the *Oxford English Dictionary*.
3. Scroll.in, "How an Indian took shampooing to England", https://scroll.in/article/768015/how-an-indian-took-shampooing-to-england . Independent confirmation: *champo* from Hindi, massage; September 1814 bathhouse in Brighton; shampooing = therapeutic massage; royal Warrants of Appointment from George IV and William IV.
