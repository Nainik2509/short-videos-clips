## Facts and sources (checked 2026-11-16)
- Racetrack Playa, Death Valley National Park (California): rocks leave long trails in the dried mud; the tracks were known for decades, but nobody had seen a rock move.
- In winter 2013–14 researchers fitted rocks with motion-activated GPS units, plus a weather station and time-lapse cameras.
- The movers: a **thin "windowpane" ice sheet, about 3–6 mm thick**, forms on a shallow pond, breaks into panels in the sun, and **light winds (about 3–5 m/s)** push the panels, which push the rocks along at roughly **2–5 metres a minute**.
- Open question (do not claim more): the paper does not claim every rock's movement is explained.
