# Why does your airplane window have a tiny hole? (Doodle Whys, Part 1)

Status: script approved by the user.

Language: English · Style: stickTales · Cast: Sticky (narrator) · Length target: ~30 s (about 85 words)

## Script (one line = one picture)
1. **Hook:** Why does your airplane window have a tiny hole?
2. Look closely. Each window has three layers, and the middle one has a hole. *(Sticky at a window, arrow to the hole)*
3. Up high, the cabin is pressurised, so the air inside pushes hard on the glass. *(cabin vs thin outside air)*
4. The hole lets that pressure reach the outer pane, so the outer pane carries the load. *(pressure arrows)*
5. That way, if a pane ever fails, it is the outer one, not the one next to you. *(outer pane highlighted)*
6. The hole also lets moisture escape, so your window stays clear. *(fog clearing)*
7. Tiny hole, big job. Next: why are airplane windows round? *(teaser)*

## Sources and fact check
1. Fortune, "Airplane window holes" (2015-06-01), https://fortune.com/2015/06/01/airplane-window-holes/ . Three panes; the "breather" or "bleed" hole is in the middle pane; it lets the outer pane bear the cabin pressure; if a pane fails it is the outer one; it releases moisture so the window stays mostly fog-free. (Fortune relays a Slate column by airline pilot Mark Vanhoenacker.)
2. Afar, "Why airplane windows have tiny holes", https://www.afar.com/magazine/why-airplane-windows-have-tiny-holes . Three layers of stretched acrylic; the hole is in the central pane; it equalises pressure between the gap and the cabin; it helps prevent condensation between the panes.
