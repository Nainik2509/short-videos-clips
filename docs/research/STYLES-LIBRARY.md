# Style library: sub-styles seen on @thesticktales3000

The channel is not one look. It uses a few distinct treatments, picked to match the topic. Each entry says what it is, which Short shows it
(local copy in `references/<id>/frames.jpg` + `transcript.json`), when to use it, and what we build ourselves.
Pattern only: never their characters, sets, scripts or voice. Reviewed 2026-11-16 from frames and transcripts (not by ear).
Main notes and the house style: `docs/research/STYLE.md`.

## 1. Contrast twist ("India vs America", "rule, then the twist")
- **Seen in:** USkDMtz7JqU (gifts), cZExh7WRPLU (3 things), YgY8Ezj8D5w (China rents pandas).
- **Look:** two worlds side by side in time: cool navy museum / flag hall for the strict side, warm saffron ghat / market for the India side. Flags wipe across the screen as the transition ("But India does...").
- **Script:** rule (35%), "but..." (turn), payoff line that mirrors the setup ("In A it becomes history. In B it cleans a river." / "Other countries send diplomats. China sends pandas.").
- **Props:** price tag, auction paddles with rupee amounts, gold coins raining, a giant "$" or a tick.
- **Our use:** facts with a clear A vs B. Warm vs cool grading, flag or colour wipe transition, mirrored last line.

## 2. Myth-bust with a diorama ("If X is the reason, why Y?")
- **Seen in:** BXRlHtpvQPU (Delhi air after Diwali), tT6b5ncvOes (the wall may not be real).
- **Look:** host character on a night rooftop / lab; cuts to explanatory 3D models: AQI gauge with moving needle, relief map with a red pin, glass dome over a low-poly city, factory smoke; for physics, glowing green particles through two slits, a red detector cube, a wireframe city that "renders" only what is looked at.
- **Script:** question hook, the common belief, a surprising fact check, the real cause, a closing line that reframes ("fix the air every day, not blame one night").
- **Our use:** science and civic "why" explainers. One animated diagram per claim, host on screen for hook and verdict only.

## 3. Nostalgia / list on a map (sticker characters on a 3D map)
- **Seen in:** oSYpnoDr0lA (childhood cartoons).
- **Look:** a cosy living room with a CRT TV, then the camera flies over a soft watercolour world map; each country is an island, items appear as white-outlined stickers on that island. Zoom out to all islands for the finale.
- **Script:** "If you grew up ..., you had a childhood ... will never understand" then a roll-call by region, then the pride line.
- **Rights warning:** they show other studios' cartoon characters. **We never show third-party characters.** Use our own icons, or plain labels, or items that are not copyrighted (foods, toys, places).
- **Our use:** "top things from your childhood / city / festival" lists, region by region, with our own sticker icons.

## 4. Short story with a twist (dramatic, warm, painted room)
- **Seen in:** RNXCbj4ttaw (the loyal dog), 60HHkHanHZ4 (buried in a Pringles can).
- **Look:** golden-hour interior with lanterns and a window moon; characters shown in shadow silhouettes at the tense moment; slow push-ins on faces; red wash light for the wrong assumption; a calm warm reveal at the end. The Pringles one is brighter: lab, factory conveyor, shop aisle, graveyard, with year stamps (1966, 2008).
- **Script:** hook that states the odd outcome ("This man is buried in a can"), the story in 6 to 8 short sentences, a last line that flips meaning ("The blood wasn't proof of a crime. It was proof of loyalty.").
- **Our use:** folk tales and true stories. Keep dark moments off-screen (silhouette, cut away); no gore. Fits the Kahani series well.

## 5. Clean science explainer (low caption, low-poly lab world)
- **Seen in:** 3iXNdopePkg (what if Earth lost oxygen for 10 seconds).
- **Look:** neat low-poly town, bedroom with a candle, a 3D Earth, a glass box of particles (blue molecules, orange ones vanishing). A flat-cap host with a moustache talks to camera; "ghost" translucent transitions between scenes. Fewer on-screen words, more demos: candle goes out, car stops, plane drops.
- **Script:** "Imagine..." hook, "but here's the twist" (you wouldn't die), then a chain of consequences each with one demo.
- **Our use:** "what if" explainers. A tabletop-experiment look: one demo object per consequence.

## 6. One-line joke (12 s)
- **Seen in:** 6d0J_4OW3Xs (Gandhi on the note).
- **Look:** one set, one prop, a single payoff frame.
- **Our use:** filler between big reels; avoid religious or real-person jokes (see CLAUDE.md rule 3). Safe versions: everyday objects, school, food.

## 7. Quiz / "what would you do?" (law and daily life)
- **Seen in:** A3k2G-jERJw (found ₹1 lakh).
- **Look:** a bright bazaar street; red X and green tick pop-ups; big red "314" law number; a spotlight cell scene for the consequence; a second character (green hair, goggles) as the foil.
- **Script:** scenario question, rule A (tick), rule B (cross), a joke about a friend, closing question for comments.
- **Our use:** legal or money everyday dilemmas. Always check the law and say "in India, under ..."; add "not legal advice".

## 8. History scale-shock
- **Seen in:** ENe-TD531CQ (the spice company).
- **Look:** placard-style place and year labels (LONDON · 1600, DELHI · 1803), crowd of thousands of tiny soldiers with a big counter, a villain in a top hat with a map and pins, a red X over the "expected" ruler and the surprise pictured instead.
- **Script:** bold claim hook, the origin, the pivot ("spices were never the end game"), the number that shocks, the one-line moral ("One company. Profit.").
- **Our use:** history reels where the number is the star. Numbers need two sources.

## 9. Serialized survival story (from @BeanieSurvives)
- **Seen in:** references `EjoNccCYN3Y` (Part 6 Short) and `LmM7TLWBWok` (S1E3 episode). Numbers and full notes: "Live numbers" and "How Beanie Survives makes its picture" in `docs/research/STYLE.md`.
- **Look:** painted landscape band inside the vertical frame with a blurred fill, "PART N" header with a gold series line, slow pans, day / night grading, off-white info cards (supplies, rules, "NO FUEL?"), black "DAY 44" title cards.
- **Script:** a survival situation, each part answers one problem and ends on a cliffhanger title; a closing question for comments; no narration needed (music, ambient sound, on-screen text).
- **Our use:** multi-part Kahani or Cartoon runs (a journey, a siege, a festival-night rescue) with a day counter. Keep content family-safe: no gore, no horror premise copied from them.

## 10. Cinematic mechanism explainer (from @RicksClips3000)
- **Seen in:** references `VS1Z_Q-Klt8` (the jar, 34.9M views) and `7xS87iActkU` (railroads, 29.8M). Numbers, shot recipe and title patterns: "Channel study: @RicksClips3000" in `docs/research/STYLE.md`.
- **Look:** a fully 3D lit world with a colour script per scene, small stick-bean characters, 35 to 45 shots in 65 s, extreme close-ups and top-down cutaways of the mechanism, depth blur, glow, a scale-reveal last shot.
- **Script:** character, obstacle, failed attempt, the mechanism shown, twist. Title states the mechanism ("How One Cart Stopped an Entire Army").
- **Our use:** history, engineering, money and psychology "how / why" reels, 45 to 65 s, in the toon3d engine. Needs one set per scene colour script, so build sets in `src/videos/<name>/sets.ts`.
- **Not ours:** the voice-over-on-a-comic Shorts (they dub another artist's comic). We never use someone else's artwork.

## Shared across all sub-styles
- Hook in sentence 1; no greeting; one idea per sentence; ~3 words/s.
- 1 to 3 word captions, heavy rounded sans, outline, key word coloured, lower third.
- A new picture every ~2 s; camera always moving; ghost / flag / light-wipe transitions.
- A closing line that mirrors or flips the opening.

## Picking a sub-style for our story
| If the topic is... | Use |
|---|---|
| A vs B, culture or money | 1 Contrast twist |
| "Why doesn't X happen?" science or civic | 2 Myth-bust diorama |
| A list of things from childhood, places, foods | 3 Map list (own icons only) |
| Folk tale, true tale with an emotional flip | 4 Story with a twist |
| "What if...?" | 5 Clean science explainer |
| Quick laugh | 6 One-liner |
| "What would you do?" | 7 Quiz |
| A big number in history | 8 Scale shock |
| A multi-part adventure with a day counter | 9 Serialized story |
| "How did X stop / win / work?" with a mechanism | 10 Cinematic mechanism explainer |
