# My style: inspired by, not a copy

Add 3–5 videos you love (`npm run refs -- <link> <link> ...`). For each, fill this in, then ask Claude:

## The rule
Borrow the *pattern* (pacing, hook type, caption style, music mood). Never copy their script, words,
footage, characters, voice or exact layout. Make it recognisably yours.

## Video 1: https://www.youtube.com/shorts/cZExh7WRPLU
Local copy deleted after the study (re-download with `npm run refs` if needed) · "3 Things India Does Better Than America" · channel: Stick Tales · 40 s, English, ~123 words (about 3 words/s).

- **Hook (first 2 seconds):** A one-line promise of a list, said straight away, no greeting: "Three things that are normal in
  India, but almost impossible in America." It sets up a comparison and a countdown, so the viewer stays to see all three.
- **Structure:** Hook, then three numbered beats, then stop. Each beat = the number, the thing, "In India…" (the easy/cheap way),
  then "In America…" (the hard/costly way). Each beat is about 10–12 s. Same 4-line pattern three times, so it is easy to follow.
- **Pacing (cut every ~N seconds, zooms, b-roll):** A new shot or camera move about every 2 s (about 20 shots in 40 s). The
  camera is always moving: push-ins, tilts down from a big sign, swings around a character. Each new beat opens on a big
  3D numbered sign ("1", "2", "3") that the camera tilts down from. Props change with every point (shop, phone stall, pharmacy,
  clinic) so no two shots look alike.
- **Characters / look:** Simple stick-bodied cartoon characters with bean heads, shown in a painted, saturated 3D town. India scenes are warm and
  busy (yellows, oranges, happy faces); America scenes are cooler and emptier (blue/grey, stressed faces, sweat drops).
  Props make the point without words: a "✓" for a paid scan, a giant "$" sign, a signal-bar icon, a receipt with "$$$".
- **Captions (size, colour, how many words):** 1–3 words at a time, big bold white with a dark outline, centred, in the lower
  third (just under the middle of the frame, clear of the bottom UI). Words are timed to the voice; the line changes on every phrase,
  never a full sentence on screen. Some words appear faded for a moment before they fill in.
- **Music / sound:** One steady voice-over at a quick, conversational pace (about 3 words/s). _Not checked by ear: I only had the transcript and frames. Listen once and note the music and sound effects here._
- **Borrow this pattern:**
  - "Three things…" list hook + numbered signs that open each beat.
  - India-vs-America contrast as a 2-step beat (easy way, then hard way) in each point.
  - A new shot every ~2 s with constant camera motion; one visual prop per idea.
  - Short caption chunks (1–3 words) timed to the voice, big and bold.
  - Ending right after the third point, no long outro.
- **Do NOT copy:** its narration and sentence wording, its characters (bean-headed figures, the red-cap / blue-beanie guys),
  its town and shop layouts, its sign designs, its camera moves shot for shot, or its voice. Use our own cast, our own sets,
  our own palette and our own voice.
- **Topic decision (user, 2026-11-16):** the same three facts (UPI, mobile data, medicines) are covered anyway, using our own
  revised script in `stories/india-vs-america-3-things/script.md` (own wording, corrected claims, sources and caveats written
  down). Facts are free to use; wording, characters and sets are not, so keep those different from this reference.

### What the 3D look actually is (frames of 6 Shorts)
- **A real 3D set, not flat 2D.** Painted, saturated rooms and streets (palace hall with red carpet and pillars, museum with spotlights, ghat with diyas, rooftop at night, colourful bazaar, 1950s living room with a TV). Real perspective, floor, depth blur, warm key light, lens-like glow on lamps and fireworks.
- **2D cartoon characters standing in that 3D world.** Bean-shaped heads with a thick dark outline, big expressive eyes, thin stick legs and arms, simple striped/printed tops, one signature hat or hair (orange cap, green spiky hair with goggles, top hat). Faces change (smug, shocked, smiling, tired). Many characters are caricatures of real figures: **we do not do that**.
- **Colour grading tells the story:** warm gold/orange for the good or Indian side, cool navy/blue for the strict or foreign side, dark purple night for drama, clear blue sky for "open/free".
- **Camera is never still.** Push-ins on faces, low-angle hero shots, a tilt down from a big sign, orbit around a character, zoom out to a map. A cut every 1.5 to 2.5 s (about 18 to 25 shots in 30 to 40 s).
- **Each idea gets one picture and one prop.** A pillar of sparkle on the gift, a museum price tag "$1,500", a bidding crowd with paddles "₹50,000", an AQI gauge needle, a glass dome over a low-poly city for "trapped air", a pile of 23,000 tiny soldiers, a red X over a wrong idea, a green tick over a right one, a big "314" for the law section.
- **Maps as 3D dioramas.** Low-poly relief map with a red location pin; or cartoon sticker characters placed country by country on a map of islands (the nostalgia short). Zoom from one country to the next while the voice lists names.
- **Floating labels pop in with the word:** a number, a place and year ("LONDON · 1600", "DELHI · 1803"), a price, a name. Small bold pill on the scene, not a subtitle.
- **Spotlight and fast-rewind icon (⏪)** as quick visual punctuation ("wait, what?").
- **Captions:** 1 to 3 words at a time, white, heavy rounded sans (Montserrat-like), thin dark outline, centred at about 1/3 from the bottom. Numbers and key words in gold or red. Next word shown faded before it fills in.

### Script and structure patterns that worked
- **Hook = a question or a bold claim in the first sentence, no greeting.** "If firecrackers are the reason, why doesn't the air clear after?" / "A spice company conquered India, and its army was bigger than Britain's." / "You found a wallet with ₹1 lakh. Keep it?"
- **Length:** 12 s joke, 30 s explainer, 40 s list. About 3 words per second, 65 to 125 words total.
- **Shapes (pick one per reel):**
  1. *Numbered list* ("3 things ..."): sign, then "In A ... In B ...", three times.
  2. *Contrast twist*: rule in the first half, "But India does something different" at 40%, one-line payoff at the end ("In America a gift becomes history. In India it helps clean a river").
  3. *Myth-bust*: "If X is the reason, why Y?" then geography or numbers, then "So no, X isn't the cause".
  4. *Scale shock*: a number that surprises (260,000 men, more than the British army), shown as a crowd.
  5. *Quiz close*: "Be honest, what would you do?" invites comments.
- **Close:** one punchy line that restates the twist, or a direct question to the viewer. No long outro.
- **Topics:** India-linked facts, laws, history, money, everyday "why" questions. The Indian-angle list/contrast got ~10x the views of the others: lean into it, but keep every claim sourced.
- **Titles:** a question or a claim with one or two emojis, 40 to 60 characters.

### Our version: "3D Stick Tales" (house style, decided 2026-11-16)
Borrowed *patterns*; ours are the cast, sets, palette, wording and voice.
- **Engine:** the `toon3d` stack in `src/toon3d` (three.js inside Remotion, ink outlines, depth blur, glow), as proven in `.claude/skills/cartoon-3d-short`. Not the flat SVG `stickTales` style any more for new reels; the SVG stick rig stays for quick tests.
- **Our cast (never theirs):** **Sticky** (narrator: blue beanie, orange vest, round white head) plus one-off stick figures with a different hat or hair each, shown at 1/4 to 1/2 frame height. No caricatures of real people or politicians; gifts, laws and companies are shown with generic figures and labelled signs.
- **Our sets:** a library of 5 to 8 reusable 3D sets built once and reused across the series: bazaar street, museum hall, rooftop at night, ghat/river, royal court, living room with TV, relief-map table, school/bus stop.
- **Our palette:** Midnight Ink `#17124A`, Saffron `#FF8A1F`, Marigold `#FFC93C`, Teal `#2EC4B6`, Paper `#FFF4DE`, same as the channel brand. Warm = good/home, cool = strict/foreign, purple night = drama.
- **Rhythm checklist for every reel:** one picture per line of script; a cut at least every 2.5 s; one prop per idea; a map or diagram at the "why" moment; one hero shot at the twist; a red X / green tick where a myth is corrected; a label pop-in for each number and place.
- **Captions:** `<Captions>` at 3 words (English) in heavy rounded sans, key word in Marigold. Inside the safe zone.
- **Do NOT copy:** their characters (orange cap boy, green-hair goggles guy, mustache narrator, top-hat villain), set layouts shot for shot, sign designs, exact scripts and facts-with-same-wording, or voice. Topics may overlap only when we add new angle, own wording and own sources (see the India vs America note above).

### How Beanie Survives makes its picture (frames of Part 6 and S1E3, 2026-11-17)
Not real 3D. It is **painted illustrated backgrounds with 2D stick figures on top**, edited like a motion comic:
- **Layout:** a 16:9 picture band in the middle of the vertical frame, the same frame blurred and darkened above and below, and a fixed header: "PART 6" in white, series and episode name in gold.
- **Characters:** two stick people with beanies (orange hat, grey-haired one with glasses), big white round heads, thin ink bodies. They are tiny in wide shots and large only for emotion beats.
- **Backgrounds:** hand-painted style city rooftops, stairwells, shops. Strong depth (looking down a street from a ledge), one light source (a lantern, a window), day to night grading (grey day, deep blue night with a warm tent glow).
- **Camera:** slow pans and push-ins on stills, plus wide high-angle establishing shots. No fast cuts; a shot holds 3 to 6 s (about 60 s of frames shows 10 to 15 distinct shots).
- **Information cards:** off-white cards with a small icon, a bold title and one line (NO FUEL?, HOW TO CROSS A GAP, SUPPLIES with water / gas / food bars), "DAY 44" black title cards between beats, an end card "Day 60 is on YouTube".
- **Sound:** no narration (Whisper found 0 words); mean level -29 dB, so quiet ambient and music with on-screen text carrying the story.

**What we borrow (pattern only):** the day counter, the "Part N" header with a gold series line, supply / status cards, the one-line cliffhanger title for each part, a Shorts cut for each long episode, and a closing "Next: ..." card.
**What we do not:** their characters (beanie couple), setting, zombie premise, scripts, card layouts, or the AI-painted background look as is. Our backgrounds stay code-drawn (SVG or toon3d), our cast Sticky or the rig characters.

### Combined takeaways for our house style
1. **Pick by goal.** Views from strangers: one-off list or contrast Short (Stick Tales pattern). Followers and returning viewers: a numbered, serialized story with a cliffhanger (Beanie pattern).
2. **Header and counter.** In series reels show "Part N" plus series name in the first second, and a day or step counter for progress.
3. **Info cards** (status, supplies, rule, number) replace long narration; keep them inside the safe zone.
4. **Two pacing modes:** fast (cut every ~2 s, 1 to 3 word captions) for lists; slow (3 to 6 s holds, pans, quiet music) for dramatic story beats.
5. **Do not trust one hit.** Judge a style by the median of several videos, not its best one.

### Two kinds of Shorts on the channel (do not mix them up when studying)
1. **Cinematic animated explainers** (the 9M to 35M ones, about 60 to 78 s). Fully original 3D worlds with stick-bodied, bean-headed characters.
2. **Voice-over on someone else's comic** ("Wanna Hear A Joke?", "Oh Nah", "Nice Try Frank"; 30 s or less, 50k to 4M views, emoji titles). Description credits the comic's artist ("I only do the voice"). **We do not do this kind**: it is another artist's work.

### Why the explainers look cinematic (frames of the jar and railroad Shorts)
- **A real 3D world, lit like a film.** Gold dragon-headed jar in a throne hall with red drapes and candle glow; a Canyon railway with orange cliff walls, deep blue water and a steam train. Each place has its own colour script: warm throne red and gold, sunset pink and orange for the journey, moonlit blue-violet for night, orange canyon plus blue sky for the railway.
- **Characters are small 2D-style stick people in that 3D world:** big white bean heads, thick ink outline, thin black limbs, one clear costume item per role (striped engineer cap, bowler hat and yellow vest, imperial hat, armour). Faces change fast (calm, shocked, furious purple with steam, joy).
- **The camera is the star.** Extreme close-ups of the object (dragon mouth with fire, a rod inside the jar), top-down shots into the mechanism, wide high-angle shots of a crowd or army, low-angle hero shots on rails, a pull back to a huge landscape; foreground objects blur in front (depth of field). About **35 to 45 different shots in 65 s, a new shot every 1.5 to 1.8 s**.
- **One picture for every phrase.** The caption word and the image match (the word "army" shows an army; "ripple" shows a ripple in a bowl). A mechanism is always shown, never only said: how the jar detects an earthquake, how rail gaps close in heat.
- **Light and glow:** warm key light, lens glow on lamps and gold, light rays at sunset, a yellow rail glowing as it heats, a volumetric mushroom cloud for the finale.
- **Captions:** 1 to 2 words at a time, small, white with dark outline, **centred in the middle of the frame (about 40 to 50% from the top)**, new word with every beat, no colour changes. They sit on the action, not on the bottom UI.
- **Sound:** mean level about -16.7 dB (louder than the -29 dB Beanie episodes): voice plus score and sound effects. Not checked by ear; listen once and note the music here.
- **Length:** 60 to 78 s for the big explainers; they are far above the 25 to 45 s of the Stick Tales hit, which shows long Shorts work when every second changes.

### Script shape (inferred from titles and frames, narration not transcribed)
Title is the hook: a *mechanism or consequence* ("How One Cart Stopped an Entire Army", "Why the Second Draw Always Wins"). Sequence: an odd setup, a character with a problem, an attempt that fails, the clever mechanism shown in close-up, the payoff, a reveal of scale (a city, an explosion) in the last shot. History, engineering, psychology and money all fit one pattern: **a character, an obstacle, a mechanism, a twist**.

### Titles, descriptions, packaging (from 60+ uploads)
- **Titles (explainers):** 4 to 8 words, Title Case, **no emoji, no hashtags, no numbers or caps shouting**. Patterns that repeat: `Why <thing> <surprising behaviour>` ("Why Sailors Carried Storm Oil"), `How <small thing> <big result>` ("How One Cart Stopped an Entire Army", "How a Mirror Rigged a Horse Race"), `The <noun> That/Who <odd verb>` ("The Ancient Jar That Warned an Empire", "The Burglar Who Only Moved Furniture"), `This <noun> <does something>` ("This Shape Saved Roller Coasters"). A curiosity gap without a lie.
- **Titles (comic dubs):** a quote or reaction plus emoji ("Oh Nah 💀", "\"You're Free Now\" 😭🤣"). Not for us.
- **Description:** for explainers it is **only the title repeated** (no hashtags, no links, no tags field). Comic dubs add a credit line for the artist. Channel About text is one friendly line ("Hey I'm Rick! Just a regular guy who loves doing voiceovers.").
- **Consistency is the brand:** one world style, one voice, one length band (about 60 to 65 s), the same caption style, a Short about every 1 to 3 days.
- **Our version:** keep their *discipline* (short title that states a mechanism, one picture per phrase, 1 to 2 word mid-frame captions, one cinematic colour script per scene), but our descriptions stay richer for discovery and credits (English line first, sources, music credit, see the playbook). Our cast, sets, voice and topics stay ours.

### Cinematic checklist for every toon3d reel (adds to the rhythm checklist)
1. One **colour script per scene** (warm throne, sunset path, moon night, canyon day) and a change of palette at the twist.
2. At least **one extreme close-up** of the key object and one **top-down or cutaway** of the mechanism.
3. A **wide establishing shot** every beat, then cut in; always a depth blur on foreground and background.
4. **Light:** one key light, glow on lamps / gold, rays at sunrise or sunset, a glow that shows heat, power or danger.
5. A new shot every **1.5 to 2 s** (35 to 45 in 65 s) when the voice runs fast; a caption word that matches the picture.
6. Mid-frame **1 to 2 word captions** (cinematic mode) or the lower third (story mode); never inside the bottom UI.
7. End on a **scale reveal** (the city, the army, the explosion) and stop. No outro.
